Offline · encrypted · sound

Messages that travel as sound.

When mobile data is cut, Whoosh passes short encrypted texts from phone to phone over near-ultrasound, relays them hop by hop, and saves them to Arkiv when any phone in range gets back online. It runs in the browser, with no Bluetooth pairing, no app store and no account.

How it works

Each phone's speaker and microphone carry the message.

  1. 01

    Share a key in person

    One phone creates a group and shows six words. Read them aloud to people you trust. They never touch a server.

  2. 02

    Send as sound

    Your text is encrypted, packed into a packet of at most 127 bytes and played as near-ultrasonic tones through ggwave.

  3. 03

    Phones relay it

    Listening phones check the tag and rebroadcast after a random delay, only once the room is quiet, until the ttl runs out.

  4. 04

    A gateway settles it

    Any phone with internet writes the ciphertext to Arkiv. A second group of phones pulls it down and plays it to the people around it.

Arkiv settlement layer

A record no single operator can quietly delete.

Groups of phones out of earshot of each other are joined through Arkiv. A gateway verifies each packet it hears, checks whether it is already stored, and writes it as a whoosh.msg entity, byte-identical to what was on the air. Readers query by channel and time, verify the GCM tag, and drop anything forged.

The payload is ciphertext. Without the group key, Arkiv shows a rotating channel tag, a time, a size and the gateway's burner wallet.

Limits

What Whoosh does not do.

  • Pager-scale. Short texts, a few metres per hop, one speaker at a time. Not a chat app.
  • Not covert. Near-ultrasound shows up on any spectrum analyser.
  • No forward secrecy. A leaked key decrypts every past message, including those on Arkiv.
  • No sender authentication. Any key holder can forge a message as any other.
  • Foreground only. Browsers do not allow background audio, so a phone relays only while the app is open.
